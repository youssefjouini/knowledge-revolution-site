> "The mind is not a vessel to be filled, but a fire to be kindled."
> — After Plutarch

This famous line is a paraphrase of a passage from **Plutarch's essay *On Listening***, written almost 2,000 years ago. His point: a student's mind does not need to be *filled* like a bottle — it needs a spark that makes it want to think by itself and look for the truth.

## Filling notebooks vs. living an experience

Too often, learning still looks like filling a vessel: slides, notes, exams, repeat. Students copy, memorize — and forget. The comments we collected from students online say it clearly: *"all they do is showing a PowerPoint that was also uploaded online and read off it."*

## Kindling a fire

What kindles the fire is different: a question you are free to ask, an answer that fits *you*, a teacher who takes the time. That is exactly what great teachers do — and exactly what most students do not get often enough.

## Our belief

Learning isn't about filling notebooks. **It's about living an experience.**

That is why our avatars are designed to *talk with* students, not *at* them: you ask, they answer, you ask again — face to face, in your own language, without judgment.
