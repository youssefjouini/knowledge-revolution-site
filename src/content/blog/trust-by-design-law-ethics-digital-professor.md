The biggest ethical risk of our project is not a wrong fact. It is a convincing face saying it. An avatar with a real professor's voice makes errors, bias and over-reliance more persuasive. That is why Part 5 of our Investigate report — the legal, ethical and market study by Youssef — matters as much as the technology.

## Six rules that shape the solution

**1. Always say it is an AI.** Since August 2026, the EU AI Act (Article 50) requires that users know they are talking to a machine, and that synthetic voices and faces be labelled.

**2. Never read the student's face.** Emotion recognition is banned in education since February 2025 (AI Act, Article 5). Our mentor will detect confusion from the conversation — messages, hesitations, repeated questions — never from a webcam.

**3. A face and a voice are biometric data.** Cloning a professor needs explicit, separate consent, a liveness check and the right to withdraw at any time.

**4. Student data stays protected.** In Tunisia, sending student or professor data to a foreign provider requires an authorisation from the INPDP.

**5. Protect minors.** An age gate and parental consent wherever minors are concerned.

**6. A human stays responsible.** Every mentor has a named owner — the professor or a curator — who reviews its content and answers the questions it should not.

## What the evidence says about trust

Only 46% of people say they trust AI. In one survey, 62% of computing students said they would not trust AI-presented videos as much as recorded ones. And in our own testing, an avatar using Albert Einstein's face was created on a commercial platform without a single authorisation step.

## Our difference

Talking avatars are already sold by the minute. What does not exist yet is the educational layer on top: an avatar of the student's own professor, grounded in their course, transparent about being an AI, built on consent — with the real professor able to step in.

> Avatars extend teachers. They never replace them.
